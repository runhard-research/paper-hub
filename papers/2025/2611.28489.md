## Notes
Vision Banana showed that a closed image generator, instruction-tuned to paint perception outputs as RGB images, becomes a generalist vision model. Does this carry over to \emph{open} generators, and if not, what fails? We answer with \textbf{Open Vision Banana (OVB)}, an open framework that adapts the 7B diffusion generator of Qwen-Image-2.1 into one instruction-driven model for metric depth, semantic, instance, and referring segmentation, using a single shared low-rank adapter and public data. Each task is a \emph{task contract} whose RGB encoder, instruction, and decoder share one specification and are checked before training. Studying 42 externally evaluated checkpoints from two 100k-update runs, six exact weight-space merges, and eight controlled shorter runs, we find that the gap to the closed system is specific and measurable. Geometry is learned early while absolute scale is not: median-aligned NYUv2 $δ_1$ stays at 0.89--0.93 throughout training as unaligned $δ_1$ wanders between 0.11 and 0.50. The scale is not committed by a single sample: a ten-seed ensemble lifts unaligned $δ_1$ from 0.348 to 0.516 without retraining. Depth and 2D segmentation compete for the shared adapter, and weight merging moves along this trade-off without escaping it. Finally, lower validation flow loss comes with lower Cityscapes accuracy (Spearman $ρ\approx-0.9$ between loss reduction and mIoU), so the loss cannot select checkpoints. We release code, contracts, training data, and all evaluation outputs.
